## 1.0.0 (2026-11-26)

Connected: OwnHealth now pulls records from US patient portals and Medicare, reads wearables through their APIs and account exports, opens hospital imaging CDs, shares a summary through a SMART Health Link, and keeps each profile's files under its own key. Upgrade in place. Take a backup first, while the compose file still names 0.4.0. Then set `admin` and `app` to `drumsergio/ownhealth:1.0.0` and `ocr` to `drumsergio/ownhealth:1.0.0-ocr`, and follow the 1.0 steps for the secrets key and the keyring folder ([UPGRADING.md](UPGRADING.md#upgrading-to-10)). Twenty-three new migrations (1200, 1201, 1202, 1203, 1210, 1220, 1221, 1230, 1231, 1232, 1233, 1234, 1249, 1250, 1260, 1270, 1271, 1280, 1290, 1291, 1292, 1298, 1299) apply at start. The rule pack stays at 0.2.0, so on an upgrade from 0.4.0 every profile's `state_hash` stays as it was. From 0.3.0 or earlier it moves once, as the 0.4.0 entry says. Passwords, passkeys, access tokens and OAuth connections keep working.

- **The secrets key is required.** Every command except `migrate` and `healthcheck` refuses to start without `OWNHEALTH_SECRETS_KEY`, and prints how to make one. Keep the key you have: it already encrypts your inbox secrets. The quickstart `compose.yaml` now reads it from the file `secrets/secrets_key`, never from `.env`. The homelab compose file also gives it to `admin` and `ocr`, so backups and restores open the encrypted files.
- **New folders before the first start.** Create `keyring/<project>` (`keyring/ownhealth` for the default project) for the profile keys, owned by UID and GID 10001 with mode 0700. The homelab file also mounts `secrets/imaging`. Both steps are in [UPGRADING.md](UPGRADING.md#upgrading-to-10).
- **Database role.** `app-role.sql` now revokes CONNECT on the `ownhealth` database from PUBLIC. A role you added yourself, such as a monitoring login, needs `grant connect on database ownhealth to <role>`.
- **Opt-in settings.** Nothing new starts by itself. The `imaging` and `wearables` compose profiles add Orthanc and Open Wearables. `OWNHEALTH_SHARE_URL` turns sharing on, through Caddy's link-only port 8090 (`OWNHEALTH_SHARE_PORT` moves it). `OWNHEALTH_OW_CALLBACK` and `OWNHEALTH_OW_PUBLIC_URL` serve public wearable callbacks. `OWNHEALTH_TIMEZONE`, `OWNHEALTH_ORTHANC_URL`, `OWNHEALTH_PARTITION_ROWS` and `OWNHEALTH_ANCHOR_DIR` are new and optional ([.env.example](.env.example)).
- **US patient portals.** Connect Epic, Oracle Health, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, MEDITECH, NextGen or VA through SMART on FHIR, with your own registered vendor app, in one wizard. OwnHealth verifies the patient, then syncs the supported records every day. Find a hospital by name. The vendor lists download only after someone starts a US connection.
- **Medicare.** Claims and coverage come in through CMS Blue Button. They land as one review item per type, and the Connections card shows the claim count of the latest pull.
- **Connections stay fresh.** A connection refreshes by itself, asks to reconnect when the vendor refuses it, and OwnHealth asks the vendor to revoke it on disconnect or delete. If that request fails, the app records the failure and shows the vendor's own revoke page, where you can revoke access yourself. The local token is deleted either way. Each vendor's token rules drive the refresh. The Connections screen shows each connection's status and sign-in end, and reconnects it in one click. A daily pull never brings back review items a person already handled. Household managers can delete a saved vendor app and its sealed secrets. Token, refresh and link requests get the same address pinning and size and time caps as every download. A busy or failing SMART server now asks the person to try again later.
- **C-CDA, health cards and links.** Upload the C-CDA file a US portal downloads. Allergies, problems, medicines, immunizations, labs, vital signs, visits, procedures and social history go through the same patient match, review and dedup as FHIR. A coded smoking status from FHIR or C-CDA is stored as a value. Social history about sexual activity, orientation or pregnancy stays private. Import a SMART Health Card from its text, file or QR photo, and a SMART Health Link a clinic sends. Each waits until you approve its issuer or host and the signature checks out. Any other signing or encryption algorithm is refused.
- **Family from GEDCOM.** Import a GEDCOM tree and review the proposed relatives. OwnHealth keeps the family facts and discards the file and its names.
- **Wearable APIs.** The `wearables` compose profile runs the Open Wearables 0.11.0 sidecar for Oura, WHOOP, Polar and Withings. Its telemetry is off, it publishes no port, and its database role cannot open the health database. OwnHealth pulls the allowlisted vendors' readings per vendor and day, and removes a deleted profile's sidecar user. A sync alert opens when a pull fails, when a vendor reports stale or revoked access, or when a vendor sends only rows OwnHealth cannot read. It closes when sync recovers. Each vendor's API terms are quoted on its own page ([docs/connectors](docs/connectors/README.md)). Polar data reach agents.
- **Account exports and files.** Upload a Garmin account ZIP, a Strava account ZIP, a Fitbit Google Takeout ZIP (legacy and current layouts), FIT activity files (`.fit`, `.fit.gz`), or a LibreView or Dexcom Clarity CSV (en, de, es). Each result lists every skipped file and its reason, and a damaged ZIP member no longer stops the rest. No GPS route is stored. A CGM CSV is read in the browser's time zone, or in `OWNHEALTH_TIMEZONE`, and keeps that zone. Health Auto Export leaves out readings dated more than a day ahead and asks the person to check the phone's clock.
- **Wearable views.** Vendor scores such as readiness and Body Battery show apart from measurements, named by vendor and connection. An HRV chart keeps one source for the whole window and names the sources it left out. A long MCP trend shows the real lows and highs of each week or month. A profile export now holds the wearable values. Before, it held only their timestamps. `ownhealth partition` splits a large sample table into monthly partitions without copying a row ([deploy/README.md](deploy/README.md#large-wearable-histories)).
- **Imaging.** The `imaging` compose profile adds Orthanc 26.11.1 with its OHIF plugin, with authentication on and no published port. Import a hospital CD folder or ZIP as one study. A study with another patient's identity or a clashing UID waits for review, outside Orthanc. Open a study from the Imaging page or the timeline, next to its report. The viewer runs sandboxed and reaches only that study. Orthanc drops a deleted profile's images and is refilled after a restore. CI checks that a 2,000-image CT CD opens in under five seconds.
- **Sharing.** Send a doctor a scoped summary through a SMART Health Link from the new Share links page, with an optional PIN, an expiry, an access log and revoke. Stock SHL viewers open it. Sharing stays off until `OWNHEALTH_SHARE_URL` is set, and Caddy's public site block exposes only the link routes ([deploy/README.md](deploy/README.md#sharing-a-summary)). A scannable emergency card gives first responders minimal offline text and an optional live summary.
- **Emergency access.** Name a relative who can ask for emergency access. It opens only after the delay has passed and an owner's calendar feed has shown the request, so naming a contact needs an owner calendar feed. The owner can cancel it in the app or from the calendar link. The owner sees each use in the app's notices.
- **Per-profile keys.** Each profile's documents, genome files and connector tokens are encrypted under its own key. Held mail and unassigned documents have their own keys too. Profile keys live in `keyring.json`, outside the database dump and restic. The keys of held mail and unassigned documents are stored in the database, so its dump holds them. Deleting a profile makes its encrypted files unreadable, also in older backups once every older keyring copy is gone. Files still in the plain layout (stored before 1.0 and not yet sealed by `seal-legacy`, or kept plain by it) stay readable in every backup that holds them, until restic retention drops that backup. The default retention keeps a monthly snapshot for 12 months. Deleting a person removes every file and stored credential they own. `ownhealth seal-legacy` encrypts files and tokens stored before 1.0, when you start it.
- **Access log.** Each access-log entry is chained to the one before it. A backup refuses to run when the database owner rewrote the log. MCP & access shows whether the log holds and who saw each profile. Wearable job results need wearable read access, and each read of them is audited. The household owner can turn on a private report of where agent calls fell short. It starts off.
- **Who sees what.** A member without the grant no longer sees a profile's source kinds and labels, a document's sensitive details and counts, or review items they cannot open. Each review page holds only items the reader may see, and `GET /api/review` no longer returns a `withheld` count. Import job details and failures follow the document's current profile and the reader's scopes. A FHIR security label on a resource, or on a whole uploaded Bundle, marks the document and its facts sensitive. Emergency access used by review counts is recorded. A person's or agent's read of `GET /api/inboxes/health` or `GET /api/inboxes/{id}/health` that shows a source now writes one access-log row per profile it shows. A read that shows no source writes none, and so does a source's own scrape token. A jobs poll that shows only state, kind and times writes none, except for a DICOM import ([server/RUST-CONTRACTS.md](server/RUST-CONTRACTS.md)). Document JSON is sent with `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. A caregiver opening Inbox no longer gets an error.
- **Backups and restore.** Upgrades from 0.1.1, 0.2.0, 0.3.0 and 0.4.0 are tested on every pull request, against databases those releases wrote. Keyless backups from v0.4.0 restore with this version, without a keyring. Restore a backup from 0.3.0 or earlier with the version that wrote it, then upgrade: rule pack 0.2.0 changed every `state_hash`, so this version's check reports each one as different. Keyed backups from untagged builds need the build that wrote them if they predate the keyring or list profile UUIDs without key IDs. Restore with that build, then take a new backup with this version. Old backups no longer reopen a deleted profile's encrypted files once every older keyring copy is gone, and plain-layout files in an older backup stay readable until retention drops it. `verify-restore` never drops a database it did not create, and it reports damaged sealed files. `restore.sh` prints and checks the snapshot it restores.
- **Household overview.** The home page shows each person's flags, overdue items and stale values on one screen.
- **Languages.** German, French, Catalan, Italian and Portuguese are available as beta languages, in the app and on every printed sheet. Adding a language is one data PR, and CI names any missing string. An unknown `lang` answers 422.
- **Fixes.** A correction saved during a doctor summary or an IPS export shows on all of it or on none of it. A shared IPS shows a lab panel once. The Done picker offers a report dated only by its report date. An agent that asks where a visit came from reads its reason and its clinic.
- **Demo.** The demo computes SCORE2 and PREVENT for Alex, and shows Lucía a family hint from the high LDL her mother shares. The demo's pressure reading is dated 2026-09-14, so from 2027-09-14 its calculators read not assessed.
- **Brand and docs.** OwnHealth has a logo, a banner, a docs site at https://geiserx.github.io/OwnHealth/ and app colours that match them. The roadmap names the test behind every 1.0 exit criterion and what 1.0 cuts.
- **CI.** Every pull request runs the UI unit tests and opens the app in a real browser. CI fails on RustSec advisories, on licences outside the AGPL allowlist, and on crashes found by fuzzing the JOSE, keyring and SHL parsers. It runs a real SMART PKCE launch with refresh and import, and the poller against a seeded Open Wearables 0.11.0.

Known limits: Withings readings stay withheld from agents and fail closed. They show on the Wearables page and its REST routes only, until its partner terms are quoted. Oura, WHOOP, Dexcom and Strava stay withheld too, each for the clause its page in [docs/connectors](docs/connectors/README.md) names. The 30-day soak measurements come after 1.0, so every US vendor still shows as not verified live. The native Dexcom API connector is not built yet. Dexcom readings come in through a Clarity CSV or Nightscout. A reconnect leaves the old refresh token alive at the vendor. Imaging viewer links live in memory, so a restart closes open viewers. Held mail and unassigned documents keep their own key in the database dump until retention removes it. The Gadgetbridge and Apple Health readers decrypt to a private temp copy (mode 0600) while they read. No API route switches the US vendor directory off. A retracted `.fit` file does not bring back a `.fit.gz` copy of the same activity. CI scans no advisories or licences for the UI's npm packages, and only `compose.homelab.yaml` is hardened. A FHIR bundle uploaded before 1.0 whose security label sits only on the Bundle itself, or only on a resource OwnHealth did not map, keeps a document that is not flagged sensitive. With the label on the Bundle itself, its facts are not flagged either. 1.0 does not rescan these older uploads. A FHIR server that adds a security label to a resource without changing its `versionId` does not flag the fact OwnHealth already stored from that version.

## 0.4.0 (2026-11-19)

Insight: the record now answers what matters in a visit, each answer cited and the same on every run. It covers medicines and supplements, calculators, screenings and vaccines due, family history, ClinVar changes and a printed doctor summary. Upgrade in place: take a backup first, then set `admin` and `app` to `drumsergio/ownhealth:0.4.0` and `ocr` to `0.4.0-ocr` ([deploy/README.md](deploy/README.md#upgrades)). Seven new migrations (1100, 1101, 1110, 1111, 1120, 1130, 1131) apply at start. Passwords, passkeys, access tokens and OAuth connections keep working.

- **Every `state_hash` moves once.** Rule pack 0.2.0 changes the pack hash and the `state_hash` of every profile, on purpose: the same data, judged by the new pack. Two runs on 0.4.0 still give the same hash. Restore a 0.3.0 backup with 0.3.0, then upgrade ([deploy/README.md](deploy/README.md#restore)). For the same reason, `restore-test.sh` on a snapshot made before the upgrade reports every profile's `state_hash` as different; run `./deploy/restic/backup.sh` once after the upgrade, so the restore test checks a 0.4.0 snapshot. Apart from the pack, a hash also moves on an install that sets `OWNHEALTH_AGE_OF_MAJORITY` to a value other than 18 and has no household setting, for a person aged between that value and 18, because every screen now uses one minor rule.
- **ClinVar download.** A household with a genome now downloads NCBI's weekly ClinVar file, about 200 MB a week. The request carries no personal data, and the file is deleted after the comparison. Turn it off in Settings, or set a local file path on a server without internet.
- **Medicines and supplements.** Medicines keep their schedule, prescriber and source codes (RxNorm, ATC or a national code, only as received). A Medications screen shows current medicines, their history, PGx badges and the drug check. A supplement can keep a photo of its label and the person's recorded decision (keep, stop, do not buy again) with a reason; the export and the history keep both. The drug check lists supplements beside medicines and quotes DPWG text from the person's genome file next to CPIC.
- **Rule pack 0.2.0.** A change is judged only within one lab and method, each marker shows the person's usual level, each reference change value cites its source by DOI, and only CYP inhibitors on the FDA table are named. An RCV sentence shows both values in one unit. The Spanish out-of-range flag says "rango lab." in place of "rango del laboratorio", so the status page stays under 2 KB in Spanish too.
- **Calculators.** eGFR by CKD-EPI 2021 (creatinine, and creatinine with cystatin C), FIB-4 and HOMA-IR from each draw. The lipid page shows 10-year cardiovascular risk by SCORE2, SCORE2-OP or PREVENT. Each result has its formula, citation and sourced inputs, or the reason it was not computed. An agent reads them as the MCP resource `ownhealth://profile/{id}/calculators`. One page per calculator: [docs/calculators](docs/calculators/README.md).
- **Due.** A Due screen and a `due` status section list the screenings and vaccines of each person's country guideline (PAPPS for Spain, USPSTF for the US) with last and next dates, each cited. A person can snooze an item to a date, mark it done with the record or document that shows it, or dismiss it with a reason, and can answer the smoking and hysterectomy questions that decide whether some rows apply. The Due screen also lists the tests worth asking for at the next blood draw, with reasons and sources, and prints them as a request. A calendar app can subscribe to one profile's due items through a private feed, made, named or revoked in Settings.
- **Missing reports.** Visits with no report on file and year-long stretches with no records show on the timeline and the Due list. A printable letter asks the provider for a copy under GDPR Art. 15 (with Ley 41/2002 art. 18 in Spain).
- **Family.** Record each relative's health history and see it as a pedigree, with names hidden by default. It exports as FHIR FamilyMemberHistory and imports back. The Family screen lists the cited patterns in the history and rings their relatives in the tree, and `get_health_status` names them in a `family` section. A relative can choose, one relative at a time, to let a high LDL or a genetic finding show as a cited hint in another family member's record. A child who reaches the age of majority takes over her own profile: guardian access ends, she sees who had it and can give it back, and every screen, agent and backup uses the same age, set per household.
- **Genome.** When ClinVar reclassifies a variant a person carries, a review item says so. It alerts only on an upgrade to P/LP with 1 star or more, a downgrade of a shown P/LP, or a change in an ACMG SF gene. The comparison runs every week, and the Genome page shows the ClinVar history and the release in use. The Genome screen shows each CPIC gene's call in a grid, marks out-of-date PGx calls as Stale with the OwnGenome command that runs them again, hides a PRS percentile outside its coverage or ancestry, and cites the 16% PPV of a rare P/LP from a chip. Two adult partners can see where both carry a variant in the same recessive gene; a 1 in 4 figure shows only when both results are confirmed. The Genome page and the drug check no longer crash on a repeated line.
- **Timeline.** Conditions and medicine courses show as bars, between visits (typed in or imported from FHIR), documents and procedures. A document opens from the timeline, and a sensitive report stays hidden without `read:sensitive`.
- **Printing and export.** One PDF writer prints every sheet with the same bytes on each run. The doctor summary prints on at most two A4 pages, in Spanish or English, for general practice, cardiology, endocrinology or hematology. Every value names its source, including eGFR, FIB-4, SCORE2 and PREVENT, the tests due, family patterns and supplement decisions. The person can download an International Patient Summary as a FHIR document Bundle and print the same summary as a PDF, and the doctor summary lists procedures.
- **Information, not advice.** The wording lint is its own CI step. It catches Spanish infinitive instructions, quotes with no source and advice in any data file, and every new data file needs an https citation. A guide explains how to add a country's screenings and vaccines as one reviewed YAML file ([docs/guidelines.md](docs/guidelines.md)), and a weekly job reports dead guideline links in one issue.
- **Roadmap.** It states what 0.4.0 ships, what moved to v1.0 or v2 and what was cut, with a reason for each.

Known limits: the demo shows no family hint and no computed SCORE2 or PREVENT, because no relative has a high LDL with a share and Alex's draw has no HDL, pressure or smoking status; the demo seed gets them in a later release, with its goldens recorded again. A lab value that a final report confirmed is exported as final, but its stored FHIR copy still says `preliminary`: clinical rows are immutable except for their status, and changing that needs a migration. The doctor summary reads its parts in separate statements, not in one snapshot, so a correction saved during a print can mix two states. The rule pack's own defaults (the recency windows for thyroid, liver, blood count and glucose, among others) cite our design doc, marked `basis: pack_default`, not an outside guideline. An IPS panel with several components is exported once per component. Measured exit criteria: [docs/v0.3-exit-criteria.md](docs/v0.3-exit-criteria.md).

## 0.3.0 (2026-11-10)

Inboxes: data now arrives without an upload, and scans become values through local OCR or your own agent. Upgrade in place by changing the image tag to `drumsergio/ownhealth:0.3.0`; four new migrations (1000, 1001, 1050, 1051) apply at start, and passwords, passkeys, access tokens and every profile's `state_hash` stay as they were. Set `OWNHEALTH_SECRETS_KEY` (`openssl rand -base64 32`) before you add an inbox that needs a password or token ([.env.example](.env.example)). The watched folder `./inbox` must be writable by the app's user: run `mkdir -p inbox && sudo chown 10001:10001 inbox` next to `compose.yaml` before you upgrade, or the folder inbox shows as failing. The default image grows from 352 MB to 364 MB unpacked (79 MB to 84 MB to download, arm64); the `-ocr` variant with the engine and models is 436 MB (109 MB to download).

- **Email.** OwnHealth polls a mailbox of its own every minute over IMAP (TLS, STARTTLS, or plain only to a private address). Mail from an allowed sender, with its own server's DMARC, SPF or DKIM pass, goes to that sender's profile; everything else waits in quarantine and is never read until a person releases it, with "allow sender" in one click. Locked lab PDFs open with passwords kept per sender, encrypted at rest.
- **Watched folder, Paperless-ngx, phone.** A scanner or synced folder (one subfolder per profile), the Paperless "Document Added" webhook with the original pulled over its API and a link back, the Android share target and an iOS Shortcut recipe. Dedup now has three levels: file hash, page fingerprint (a scan or photo of a report you hold is flagged "probably the same" once OCR has read its words; the picture alone never decides, since every report from one lab looks alike), and the observation match.
- **OCR** in the `drumsergio/ownhealth:0.3.0-ocr` image and the `ocr` compose profile: PP-OCRv5 through ONNX Runtime, in Rust, on CPU. Row F1 0.954 on the scanned and photographed eval set at under a second a page ([docs/ocr-benchmark.md](docs/ocr-benchmark.md)). Every OCR value is checked against the page's words. The default image carries no models; the `-ocr` image ships the licence texts of PaddleOCR (the models) and ONNX Runtime under `/usr/share/doc/`.
- **Agent extraction.** Without OCR, scans wait as pending documents. `list_pending_documents` and `get_document_page` let your own agent read them over MCP and `propose` values with page and box; ungrounded values are flagged (a value counts only on a line that prints its analyte's name, and inside the box when the agent gives one), and the review queue shows which documents of that profile were in the agent's context. A page nothing was extracted from (queued for OCR, its pending item dismissed) needs every read category and the agents switch, like a pending page ([docs/agent-extraction.md](docs/agent-extraction.md)).
- **OAuth 2.1 for MCP.** Protected-resource metadata (RFC 9728), audience checks (RFC 8707), PKCE S256 and Client ID Metadata Documents, so claude.ai and Claude Code connect with no client registration. A native client's loopback redirect matches on any port (RFC 8252); a client may register only https, loopback http or a reverse-domain app scheme as its redirect. A refused authorization request is shown as an error page, never redirected, so `/oauth/authorize` cannot be used to bounce people to another site; open requests are capped and lapsed ones purged, so the unauthenticated endpoint cannot fill the database; a full cap drops the oldest requests, so a flood cannot lock a new connection out. A tool outside the scopes you approved answers `scope_missing`; to widen access, connect the client again and tick more scopes. OAuth runs only behind https.
- **Wearables and CGM.** Health Auto Export push (JSON v2), Gadgetbridge database import, Nightscout pull (v1 and v3, FreeStyle Libre through nightscout-connect), and a CGM summary per the HL7 CGM IG: time in ranges, mean glucose, GMI, and the 70% wear validity rule over 14 days. Wear counts the readings against the sensor's interval, so a sensor read every minute whose timestamps drift a few seconds still shows full wear. Resting heart rate pushed from west of UTC stays on its own day, and a raw push is kept 30 days, then removed (its samples stay). A Gadgetbridge export owns every sample it holds, so deleting an older export keeps what a newer one still has, and deleting the newest imports the older ones again; times before 2000 or more than a day ahead are skipped as `bad_time`, and a large export is written in batches. Each push or poll rebuilds only the rollups near the times it wrote, not the source's whole history.
- **Inbox screen and health.** Sources, last run, errors, quarantine and push tokens in one screen, in Spanish and English; `GET /api/inboxes/{id}/health` with the last successful sync and whether the last run was ok, partial or failed, also as Prometheus text, for alerting ([deploy/README.md](deploy/README.md#monitoring-the-inboxes)). A run where every item failed counts as a failure, and skipped attachments or points show as a partial run, never as silence.
- **Safer inbox settings.** Changing where a mailbox, Paperless or Nightscout connects needs a fresh step-up and the password or token again, so a stolen session cannot point a stored secret at another server. "Allow sender" needs the mailbox's trusted receiving server to be set. Removing a sender needs the same right and step-up as adding one, and "allow sender" on a release refuses a sender already listed for another profile. A Paperless or Nightscout on a public address must use https, so its token never crosses the internet in clear. A forged envelope sender with a quoted `;` cannot add a DKIM pass to the trusted Authentication-Results header, a stray quote in it cannot hide a failure, and a held message with two From addresses shows both. A folder source reads a profile's folder only when its creator manages that profile, and its subfolder cannot point at `.done`, `.failed`, another profile's folder or a folder another folder source already reads; the demo never reads the folder.
- **Review and Inbox screen fixes.** An agent's lab values show row by row next to the page, each with its "found on the page" flag; only rows found on the page start ticked, and Accept writes only the ticked rows. Editing an inbox keeps the settings the form does not show (Paperless tag routing, Nightscout backfill, a mailbox CA file). Every inbox can get a token, a polled one for its monitor. Replacing a live token, removing a sender and deleting a PDF password each ask for a second click. Mail held because the mailbox has no trusted authserv-id reads "not authenticated" in amber, not "failed DKIM/SPF". An incomplete CGM summary shows no time-in-range figures instead of 0 %. The UI cannot be framed by another site.
- **Nothing silently stuck.** A mailbox whose done folder cannot be used fails its run and names the folder, and new mail is read before handled mail left behind. A watched-folder file stays in place when OwnHealth itself fails (database restart, full disk) instead of going to `.failed`, and a file that vanishes mid-scan or turns into a pipe no longer stops the poll. Paperless pulls also fetch documents tagged in bulk, whose modified date does not change. A locked PDF that came before its password opens as soon as the password is added.
- **Scans keep their values.** A scanned report moved to another profile, or uploaded again after a retract, is read by OCR again for its new owner, instead of losing its values. A page OCR cannot read joins the document's pending item next to the pages it read without a table, each with its own reason. A value already stored is cited from a new document only when the row passes every check and the document names this person; otherwise it waits in review. A result printed with a date and no time and the same result with its local time are compared one UTC day either side, whichever arrives first, so the same draw is not stored twice; two results with dates only are compared only on the same date.
- **Deleting a profile always works.** A duplicate value linked from a second source, or an OAuth connection that named the profile (live or revoked), no longer blocks the delete. Connections drop the profile, and one left with no profile is revoked.
- **Every screen on a phone, in your language.** Each screen fits a phone (390 px) and a tablet (820 px) with its actions and status in view: a guardian can reveal a hidden genome finding, Revoke stays on screen, and the dashboard cards no longer overlap. Spanish and English are complete: errors, audit rows, variant lookups and flag texts read in the UI language, and Spanish numbers use a decimal comma. Empty, loading and error states say what happened and what to do; a file sent again after a failed import says that the import failed, not "Already imported".
- **Access changes confirm first.** Revoking a token, a grant or a connected app, removing a passkey and making new recovery codes each ask in the row. A wrong password keeps its dialog open with the reason. Keyboard focus stays where it was after a switch or a cancelled confirm, and the first Tab reaches the skip link. MCP & access counts the apps connected over OAuth, lists all ten tools with their scopes, and its audit log opens on agent calls (`GET /api/audit?via=mcp`), so page views no longer push them off the first page.
- **Clearer answers for agents and the CLI.** `check_drug` with an unknown drug answers `drug_unknown` with a hint, not `internal_error`. An agent sees the same days, units and review count as the person. A typo, a future date, a wrong birth date or a wrong country is refused, not saved. The CLI names the database setting it needs and reads `OWNHEALTH_DATABASE_URL_FILE`; backups are readable by their owner only. A new release reaches the browser without a hard reload, and health answers are never cached.
- **Recipes** for email, Paperless-ngx, scanner to folder, Health Auto Export, Gadgetbridge, Nightscout, the iOS Shortcut and the claude.ai connector ([docs/recipes](docs/recipes/README.md)).

Known limits: no IMAP IDLE (a poll a minute); the text body of a mail is kept in the stored message, not imported as its own document; HEIC photos are not read (the Shortcut converts to JPEG); Paperless gets no tag written back. ## 0.2.0 (2026-11-08)

The backend is now Rust: one binary with the web UI built in, in place of the Python 3.13 app. Same features, same data. Upgrade in place by changing the image tag to `drumsergio/ownhealth:0.2.0`; there are no new migrations, so a 0.1.1 database is already up to date, and passkeys, passwords, sessions and access tokens keep working. The image went from 586 MB to 348 MB unpacked (129 MB to 79 MB to download, arm64).

- **Same answers.** The REST API, the MCP tools, their errors and scopes, the cookies, the blob layout and the `state_hash` of every profile are the ones 0.1.1 gave. A black-box suite of 79 tests with answers recorded from 0.1.1 passes on both releases.
- **Stack.** axum and tokio, sqlx for Postgres, rmcp (the official Rust MCP SDK), PDFium through pdfium-render for lab PDFs, noodles for the VCF and its tabix index, webauthn-rs for passkeys.
- **Re-imports.** A lab PDF read by 0.2.0 records `pdfium` as its extraction engine, so forcing a re-import of a document 0.1.1 read adds a new extraction run. Its rows are the same.
- **Uploads.** A form field other than a file is refused past 1 MiB, and a form past 1000 fields or 1000 files, as 0.1.1 did.
- **Logs.** One line per request with method, route, status and latency, never the query string. Upload leftovers in `/data/blobs/tmp` that a killed process left behind are removed after an hour.
- **Homelab backups.** The restic repository and its S3 keys move from `.env` to `restic.env`, which only the `admin` service reads, so the app container no longer holds the keys to the backups. An install that keeps them in `.env` still works; move the lines to take them away from the app ([deploy/README.md](deploy/README.md#homelab-setup)).

## 0.1.1 (2026-11-08)

Fixes from a full CodeRabbit review of 0.1.0, plus places where the code disagreed with the design docs. No migrations; an existing 0.1.0 install upgrades in place.

- **Security.** Adding a passkey or linking an OIDC provider needs a fresh login; the OIDC callback only counts in the browser that started it; a caregiver can no longer take over a managed profile with a claim invite; document search no longer matches or quotes sensitive pages without `read:sensitive`; document bytes are sent `no-store`; a fact can no longer create a source on another profile.
- **Genome.** Lookups no longer reveal that a withheld finding sits at a position; minors are also shielded from carrier-panel genes; the importer recomputes fact ids, caps confidence per assay, checks imputed sites and keeps sensitive facts inside their gene spans; drug guidance comes from the newest CPIC version.
- **Drug check and status.** CYP2D6 phenoconversion bands follow CPIC's open bounds, so no activity score is left without a phenotype; medication matching uses whole words; `get_health_status` stays under 2 KB with long notes; `check_drug` accepts its own paging cursor.
- **Data.** Duplicate detection considers method, comparator and source; the received UCUM unit is kept like the received LOINC code; trend statistics never mix units.
- **Robustness and UI.** Oversized limits and windows answer 422 instead of 500; a failed export leaves no file behind; browser reads are audited as `ui`; a new relative's profile starts with a guardian (profiles created on 0.1.0 may still have none); UI fixes across the review queue, genome, settings and charts.

## 0.1.0 (2026-11-08)

The first release you can run: `docker compose up` with one app image and Postgres 17.

- **Imports.** Lab PDFs with a text layer (rows read with page and position, coded to LOINC, units converted, checked against the printed range, auto-accepted only when every check passes, else the review queue). Apple Health `export.zip`, FHIR R4 and IPS bundles, manual entry, and OwnGenome `genome-facts.json` with its VCF. Scans and photos wait as pending documents for an agent to read over MCP.
- **Status.** One card per body system (OK, Watch, Out, Stale, No data) from a cited YAML rule pack, with a reproducible `state_hash`. Drug check with CPIC level A quotes, current medications, relevant labs and phenoconversion.
- **MCP** at `/mcp`: `get_health_status`, `search_observations`, `get_trend`, `get_genome_findings`, `lookup_variant`, `check_drug`, `get_source`, `propose`. Hashed scoped PATs, scope-filtered `tools/list`, `untrusted_text` on free text, agent writes only into the review queue, rate limits, audit log.
- **Security.** Passkeys with recovery codes, step-up, household roles, full export (FHIR NDJSON plus originals) from the UI only.
- **UI.** Dark theme, Spanish and English, built to match the mockup.

Known limits: no curated rsID index yet, so rsID lookups outside your own facts file answer `rsid_unknown`; lab coverage is the synthetic eval set; no OCR (v0.2). Measured exit criteria: [docs/v0.1-exit-criteria.md](docs/v0.1-exit-criteria.md).
